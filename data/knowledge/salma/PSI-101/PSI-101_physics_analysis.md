# PSI-101: Physics analysis

## PSI-101 | 1. Physical question
SAME-R is the reflight of the original SAME investigation. Its primary objective is to quantify the sizes that liquid smoke particulate reaches in low gravity, and to relate the droplet size distribution to the smoke generation and smoke transport conditions in the experiment.

## PSI-101 | 2. Why a reflight
- In SAME, after the thermal precipitators returned to the ground, all the TEM grids were found severely contaminated with unidentified foreign particles.
- The grids were unusable, although some showed shapes of certain types of smoke particles.
- Based on that experience, SAME-R introduced modifications covering new materials, extended operating conditions, and diagnostic equipment.

## PSI-101 | 3. Hypothesis
The record states that the three hypotheses of the original SAME also apply to SAME-R. As written in the record:
- In microgravity, without strong buoyancy-induced flows, smoke particle growth from a pyrolyzing or smoldering source (through nucleation, condensation, and coagulation) leads to larger particles than in 1g.
- Larger particles are favored by higher expected vapor concentration and longer smoke residence time near the source, because velocities and mixing are reduced in microgravity.
- Liquid aerosol smokes produced in microgravity are expected to show substantially larger droplet sizes than under the same conditions in normal gravity, with a corresponding decrease in particle number density for the same mass of smoke.
- The change in size distribution and number density affects the response of smoke detectors, and may cause some detectors not to alarm when exposed to this smoke.

## PSI-101 | 4. Proposed approach (the solution)
SAME-R used the same hardware that SAME used on-orbit, with three modifications:
1. **New test material:** Pyrell (an intumescent fire-retardant foam by Foamex) replaced the DBP samples.
2. **Broader test conditions:** higher heating temperatures, and no flow during sample heating time.
3. **New instrument:** M-pass, developed in-house at GRC, replaced one of the two Commercial Diagnostic Enclosures. Those enclosures include a DustTrak and an ionization detector, as used in SAME.

## PSI-101 | 5. Results and outputs stated in the record
- Flight tests ran from July 16 to September 9, 2010.
- 66 test points were completed successfully.
- After the flight tests, three major post-flight ground tests were performed:
  - to improve the data reduction process,
  - to validate the lognormal distribution assumption, which is the key to the moment method used by SAME and SAME-R.
- [N/A] The record gives no measured particle sizes, number densities, or detector responses.
- [N/A] The record does not say whether the TEM contamination seen in SAME was resolved in SAME-R.
- [N/A] The record does not report the outcome of the three ground tests.

## PSI-101 | 6. Impacts
- Together with the SAME results, SAME-R can lead to technology for an advanced fire detector for future long-duration spacecraft.
- It provides quantitative data on the sensitivity of detectors to reduced-gravity smokes, allowing evaluation of the adequacy of existing technologies with relevant data.
- The Fire Prevention, Detection, and Suppression (FPDS) program plan allows re-evaluation of future sensor technology.
- It provides the reduced-gravity baseline data against which future detection technology can be evaluated.
- Earth: smoke detectors developed from these results can be useful in other extreme environments, such as submarines or underwater laboratories, where accurate smoke detection can save lives.

## PSI-101 | 7. Related experiment
- SAME (original investigation): SAME-R reuses its hardware and its three hypotheses, and follows up on its TEM-grid contamination problem.
